          What is a CSS selector and how do I get one?
          1. Right-click the status text on the page and choose Inspect.
          2. In the highlighted line, prefer id, data-testid or aria-label over generated class names like .css-1x2y3z.
          3. Write it as #the-id, [data-testid="the-value"] or [aria-label="The label"].
          4. Paste it below and click Test on current tab. Separate fallbacks with commas; they are tried in order.

          Why allow site access?

          Your browser grants access to pages on the approved site, not just one status field. Tabkeeper uses your selectors on matching tabs to read status text or form values for group titles. Nothing is sent to the developer.

          AWS Console tabs

          Puts every AWS Console tab into a tab group named after the account and role you are signed in as, read from the console's navigation bar once the page has loaded. Tabs for the same account and role share a group in each window. Switching roles moves the tab to the matching group. The sign-in page itself is not grouped.

          Why allow AWS site access?

          Enabling, testing or granting access requests https://*.console.aws.amazon.com/*, covering the console and its regional and account subdomains. Your browser grants page access across these sites.

          Tabkeeper reads account, role and user labels from the console navigation, plus the region from the URL, to name tab groups. It does not call AWS APIs or use AWS access keys. Nothing is sent to the developer.

          You can decline or revoke access; AWS account grouping needs it, but closing and basic grouping do not. Permissions and privacy details

          The extension cannot read the AWS Console yet. Grant access, or if you already did, check that your browser allows this extension to read AWS Console pages.

          Permissions & privacy

          Tabkeeper reads open-tab titles and URLs to match your rules, even without site access. Reading status fields or AWS labels inside a page needs your separate approval. You can remove that approval in your browser's extension settings.

          Rules and settings use Chrome Sync if enabled. The last 100 closed-tab titles and URLs, plus unfinished forms, stay on this device. Status and account labels are used in group titles and temporary session state.

          No analytics, ads or developer data collection. Tabkeeper makes no network requests. Avoid putting secrets in rules or choosing status selectors that read sensitive fields. Read the full permissions and data explanation
